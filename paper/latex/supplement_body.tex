\section{Latent Objective and Public Schema}

The hidden objective is a structured latent state rather than a single class
label. It combines an archetype, a four-channel weight vector, a primary KPI,
and discrete operating attributes such as risk posture, planning horizon,
segment focus, and governance strictness. Table~\ref{tab:archetypes} gives the

In the frozen configuration, shifts occur with probability $0.6$; shift steps
are sampled from $\{3,4,5\}$; and the shift can be abrupt or drift-like. The
public schema exposes recurring customer accounts, stakeholders, internal
teams, market context, and governance constraints, while redacting the latent
channel decomposition and hidden utility weights.

Let $x_t$ denote the hidden environment state at step $t$, let
$o_t = \psi(x_t)$ be the public observation exposed to the model, and let
$a_t \in \mathcal{A}$ be a structured action emitted through the benchmark's
action schema. An episode is a partially observed trajectory
\[
\tau = (o_0, a_0, o_1, a_1, \ldots, o_T).
\]
The hidden objective state is $g_t$, which determines which latent business
outcomes matter at time $t$. The agent never observes $g_t$ directly.

\paragraph{Latent utility.}
The environment maintains a normalized four-channel metric vector
$m_t \in [0,1]^4$ over growth, retention, revenue, and efficiency. The active
latent objective induces a weight vector $w_t \in \Delta^4$. The latent utility
used by the graders is
\[
U_t = \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]} \left( \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} m_{t,c} \, w_{t,c} \right),
\qquad
\mathcal{C} = \{\text{growth}, \text{retention}, \text{revenue}, \text{efficiency}\}.
\]

When a hidden shift is present, the active objective changes from a base
objective $g^{(0)}$ to a shifted objective $g^{(1)}$ at step $t_\star$. The
implementation supports abrupt shifts and drift shifts. If the shift is
abrupt, $w_t = w^{(1)}$ for all $t \ge t_\star$. If the shift is a drift over
duration $d$, then the active weight vector is a normalized convex blend
\[
w_t = \mathrm{Normalize}\!\left((1-\rho_t)w^{(0)} + \rho_t w^{(1)}\right),
\qquad
\rho_t = \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{t-t_\star+1}{d}\right).
\]
The shift is never explicitly announced to the model; it must be inferred from
changed consequences and public operating evidence.

For the three paper-facing environments, the overall score is a weighted combination of
final latent utility $U$, adaptation $A$, coherence $C$, and constraint
satisfaction $K$:
\[
S_{\mathrm{P1}} = 0.50\,U + 0.25\,A + 0.15\,C + 0.10\,K,
\]
\[
S_{\mathrm{P2}} = 0.4634\,U + 0.2927\,A + 0.1220\,C + 0.1220\,K,
\]
\[
S_{\mathrm{P3}} = 0.6364\,U + 0.2614\,A + 0.0568\,C + 0.0454\,K.
\]

Let $q_t \in [0,1]$ be the normalized decision quality at step $t$, defined as
agent decision value divided by oracle decision value under the \emph{active}
objective. After a shift, the implementation also computes a stale-objective
quality $\tilde{q}_t$ whenever the base objective remains well-defined. The
first detected adaptation step for P1 and P2 is
\[
t_{\mathrm{det}} = \min \left\{ t \ge t_\star :
q_t \ge \max(0.72, \tilde{q}_t + 0.05) \right\},
\]
with the fallback rule $t_{\mathrm{det}} = T$ if no post-shift step crosses the
threshold. The remaining adaptation terms are
\[
R = \frac{1}{|D|} \sum_{t \in D} q_t,
\qquad
\mathrm{speed} = 1 - \frac{t_{\mathrm{det}} - t_\star}{\max(T - t_\star + 1, 1)},
\]
where $D = \{t_{\mathrm{det}}, \ldots, T\}$ if detection occurs and
$D=\varnothing$ otherwise. For P1 and P2, the adaptation score is
\[
A = 0.55 \cdot \mathrm{speed} + 0.45 \cdot R.
\]

P3 uses the same final weighted-score framework but a shorter-horizon
adaptation rule. Its detected adaptation step uses threshold
\[
q_t \ge \max(0.60, \tilde{q}_t + 0.02),
\]
with the same fallback of no post-detection recovery when the threshold is not
crossed. Let $R_7$ be mean post-detection decision quality, let
$\mathrm{speed}_7$ be the normalized detection-speed term over the post-shift
window, and let $C_{\mathrm{post}}$ be trajectory coherence over post-shift
actions. Then
\[
A_7 = (0.40 \cdot \mathrm{speed}_7 + 0.60 \cdot R_7) C_{\mathrm{post}}.
\]
When an episode has no hidden shift, the adaptation component is marked not
applicable and the weighted score is renormalized over the active components
only. When a shift exists but no post-shift decision row is available,
adaptation is scored as 0.0.

Trajectory coherence is the mean within-regime cosine similarity between
successive action-strategy embeddings, ignoring the single pivot induced by a
true objective shift. To separate latent-policy failure from interface failure,
the paper also reports
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{shell}} = S_{\mathrm{native}} - S_{\mathrm{overall}},
\qquad
\Delta_{\mathrm{shift}} = S_{\mathrm{unshifted}} - S_{\mathrm{shifted}},
\]
where $S_{\mathrm{native}}$ is the mean score over episodes that remain fully
native to the structured action interface and $S_{\mathrm{overall}}$ includes
fallback-affected episodes.

\section{Action Interface and Failure Taxonomy}

The action interface is intentionally narrow. On P1 and P2, a valid action
contains a task id plus a JSON-like payload with four core keys:
\texttt{chosen\_initiatives}, \texttt{messaging\_action},
\texttt{pricing\_change\_pct}, and \texttt{support\_policy}. Optional
rationale, memory, and belief notes may also be present. The allowed messaging
values are growth push, retention campaign, revenue upsell, cost comms, and
none. The allowed support policies are premium SLA, balanced triage,
automation first, and incident swarm. P3 uses the same action envelope but
replaces initiative/package selection with \texttt{budget\_allocations}.
Validation rejects irrelevant cross-task fields.

\section{Threats to Validity}

\subsection{Provider heterogeneity}

Most frozen runs are local Ollama runs, while GPT-OSS-120B is served through
OpenRouter. We therefore treat GPT-OSS-120B as an external anchor and not as a
clean within-family local scaling point.

\subsection{Interface confounding}

Some large-model drops are clearly interface-mediated. Rather than hiding that
fact, the paper makes it part of the result and reports strict overall score,
native-only score, fallback rates, and the shell-controlled diagnostic.

\subsection{Adaptation metric sharpness}

The adaptation score for P1 and P2 uses a thresholded behavioral detection
rule. That choice is operationally meaningful, but it also makes some slices of
the benchmark harsher than a fully smooth scalar metric would. The supplement

\subsection{Synthetic world construction}

LatentGoalOps is synthetic by design. The claim is not that it replicates every
real operating environment; it is that it isolates a capability that existing
benchmarks often blur: hidden-objective tracking with delayed effects and
silent non-stationarity. The construct-validity argument therefore relies on
heldout splits, leakage audits, task design transparency, and baseline
separation rather than on realism language alone.
